**Project:** Thomas’s video game project hub (alt-history fiction on real geography)  
**Scope:** Geographic / built-environment research for Memphis, TN (east of Mississippi) and West Memphis, AR (west), appearance and layout ~1945–1950  
**Playable footprint (game):** ~20 sq mi total (≈10 east / ≈10 west); fiction uses **only Harahan Bridge** as river crossing

## 1. Executive summary

- In 1945–1950 the Mississippi at Memphis was crossed by **three** spans clustered near the south-central riverfront: the **Frisco Bridge** (1892, railroad), the **Harahan Bridge** (1916, twin-track railroad plus side “wagonways” for road traffic), and—from **17 Dec 1949**—the **Memphis & Arkansas Bridge** (dedicated highway span, later I-55). The Hernando de Soto / I-40 bridge did **not** exist until 1973. ([Tennessee Encyclopedia](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/mississippi-river-bridges/); [Encyclopedia of Arkansas – Big River Crossing / Harahan](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/big-river-crossing-12235/); [Wikipedia – Memphis & Arkansas Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_&_Arkansas_Bridge))
- **Fiction simplification:** Using **only Harahan** as a playable crossing is a deliberate alt-history choice. Historically, Frisco carried rail continuously; Harahan carried rail + road (wagonways still open through the late 1940s, then lightly used after 1949 and closed to autos by mid-1950s); Memphis & Arkansas opened late in the window (Dec 1949).
- East bank playable core should center on **downtown Memphis + riverfront**: bluff city grid (Front / Main / Second / Third / Madison / Beale / Union / Monroe), Cotton Row warehouses, South Bluffs warehouse / rail distribution, Wolf River Harbor industrial shoreline (Uptown / north downtown), Court Square, Handy Park, Riverside Drive.
- Landmark HQ **Sterick Building**: Gothic Revival skyscraper at northeast corner of Madison Ave & N. Third St (today B.B. King Blvd); construction began 1928; completed/opened ~1929–1930; ~29 stories; period nickname “Queen of Memphis.” ([Memphis Heritage](https://www.memphisheritage.org/historic-properties/sterick-building/); [Wikipedia – Sterick Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterick_Building))
- West bank: **West Memphis** (incorporated 1927) grew rapidly (pop. 895 in 1930 → 3,369 in 1940 → 9,112 in 1950) as a highway/rail/lumber/cotton town on floodplain behind the St. Francis levee. Commercial spine: **Broadway Ave (U.S. 70)** east–west and **Missouri St (U.S. 61)** north–south. ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas – West Memphis](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/west-memphis-crittenden-county-871/); AHPP Walks through History scripts)
- Landmark HQ **West Memphis City Hall**: **FACT correction** — not ~1930. PWA-era building at **100 Court Street**, planned 1938, completed **13 Jun 1939**, opened **18 Jul 1939**; housed city offices, fire station, and jail. Courtroom addition 1954–55 (post-period for Ch1 if set early). ([NPS NR nomination summary via Living New Deal / AHPP](https://livingnewdeal.org/sites/city-hall-west-memphis-ar/); [NPS asset summary](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/608d57ad-0fc3-431d-a7b6-7c80f3b69350))
- Crittenden County remained a **cotton plantation / tenant-farm** landscape; riverside north of the West Memphis commercial core (toward **Mound City** / levees) is historically plausible farmland bordering the river for a Ch1 farm set-piece.
- East-shore docks/warehouses opposite that north-west farm align with **Wolf River Harbor / Uptown** industrial waterfront (grain, hardwood, cotton warehousing, cottonseed oil, rail yards)—not the modern Mud Island park (park is 1980s).
- Period hangouts with strong documentation: **Beale Street** nightlife (Palace Theatre, clubs); **A. Schwab** dry goods/hardware on Beale; West Memphis **8th Street** nightlife (“Beale Street West”) and documented cafés (e.g., Square Deal Café / Miss Annie’s Place).
- Primary maps to chase next: **West Memphis Sanborn 1938 & Jun 1949** (Library of Congress); **Memphis Sanborn** vols through Nov 1950; **USGS Memphis topo (1939 survey / 1944 pub)**; Polk city directories; Crittenden / Shelby historical societies.

## 2. River crossings 1945–1950 (Harahan + others; rail vs road)

### Inventory (FACT)

| Bridge | Opened | Mode in 1945–50 | Notes |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **Frisco Bridge** (aka Great Bridge at Memphis) | 1892 | **Railroad only** | First Mississippi span south of St. Louis; purchased by St. Louis–San Francisco Ry 1903; still active rail in period. ~200 ft south of Harahan. Source: [TN Encyclopedia](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/mississippi-river-bridges/) |
| **Harahan Bridge** | Rail 14 Jul 1916; wagonways opened Sep 1917 | **Rail (2 tracks) + one-way wagonways** bolted on each side | Named for IC RR president J.T. Harahan; built by Rock Island / Cotton Belt / Iron Mountain joint venture; wood-plank auto roadbeds; Arkansas approach was a long viaduct over bottomland from St. Francis Levee. Fire damaged eastbound roadway 1928. By 1940s auto capacity inadequate. After Memphis & Arkansas opened (1949), wagonways used only infrequently (tractors/farm equipment); closed 1954. Sources: [Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/big-river-crossing-12235/); [TN Encyclopedia](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/mississippi-river-bridges/) |
| **Memphis & Arkansas Bridge** | **17 Dec 1949** | **Highway only** (4 lanes + sidewalks); later I-55 | Authorized 1939; construction delayed by WWII + 1945 strike; construction began **12 Sep 1945**; designed by Modjeski & Masters; piers aligned with Frisco/Harahan for navigation. Just south of Frisco. Sources: [TN Encyclopedia](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/mississippi-river-bridges/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_&_Arkansas_Bridge); [Memphis Heritage](https://www.memphisheritage.org/historic-properties/memphis-arkansas-bridge-interstate-55-bridge-ct0108/) |
| **Hernando de Soto Bridge** | 1973 | N/A for period | I-40; **omit from 1945–50 map**. |

### Fiction vs fact (game design)

- **Useful for fiction:** Restricting the player to **Harahan only** collapses three historical spans into one chokepoint—clear for stealth / escort / chase design. Harahan’s mixed rail + wooden wagonways are visually and tactically rich (narrow one-way auto decks, rail deck, long Arkansas viaduct over floodplain).
- **FACT to acknowledge in world bible:** Frisco always present as rail-only neighbor; from late Dec 1949 a modern 4-lane road bridge exists south of the cluster. If Ch1 is set **1945–mid-1949**, omitting Memphis & Arkansas is historically clean for *road* traffic (Harahan wagonways were the auto crossing). If set **1950**, fiction must either ignore the new bridge or explain its absence (war damage / unfinished / blocked—alt-history).
- Ferry traffic: historically important before Harahan wagonways; by mid-1940s bridges dominated. **UNVERIFIED** for exact 1945–50 ferry schedules at this crossing—do not invent named ferry landings without directory/map confirmation.

## 3. Memphis (East) — ~10 sq mi riverside footprint

### Suggested playable footprint (design guidance, not a surveyed boundary)

Prioritize a north–south strip along the river: roughly **Wolf River Harbor / Uptown → CBD / Cotton Row → South Bluffs / bridge approaches**, inland to about Main–Third–and a few blocks east (Beale corridor). That keeps density high within ~10 sq mi without requiring Midtown or the municipal airport.

### Districts & key streets (FACT — verified names)

- **Central Business District / bluff grid:** Front Street (river side), Main Street, Second, Third (period name; later B.B. King Blvd), Madison, Court, Union, Monroe, Gayoso, Beale. Riverside Drive along the riverfront (WPA-era improvements documented late 1930s).
- **Cotton Row / Front Row:** Front Street between roughly **Monroe and Gayoso**—cotton brokerage, factors, insurers, warehouse reps; multi-story late-19th / early-20th commercial buildings. Memphis Cotton Exchange building at Union & Front (moved mid-1920s to 65 Union). Sources: [Commercial Appeal / bicentennial Cotton Exchange coverage](https://www.chieftain.com/story/news/local/memphis200/2019/04/22/memphis-bicentennial-memphiscotton-exchange-mississippi-delta/3331133002/); [NPS Front Row HD nomination excerpt](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/f947bfb7-fbc1-4a05-8663-7ff2c72106ce/)
- **South Bluffs warehouse district:** South of Beale toward Calhoun / Tennessee St; rail + steamboat distribution warehouses; includes **Linden Station / Reichman-Crosby** complex at Wagner Place (1905–07 / 1922)—described as remaining cornerstone of the railroad/steamboat distribution center. Source: [Memphis Heritage – Linden Station](https://www.memphisheritage.org/historic-properties/linden-station-reichman-crosby-warehouse-beale-street-landing-ellis-building-bell-building-linden-building/); [NPS South Bluffs Warehouse HD](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/8f811160-bc4b-4b01-9e9e-362761d59bb8)
- **Wolf River Harbor / Uptown (north of CBD):** Through ~1950s, northern/central harbor shoreline was center for hardwood processing, grain storage/transport, cotton warehousing, cottonseed oil, stockyards, soap manufacturing; Illinois Central Northern Rail yard between Henry and Cedar Streets (post–Civil War origin). Downtown harbor section included Navy Yard Compress (cotton oil, steel, warehousing) and rail yard Adams–Winchester. Source: [Shelby County Wolf River Harbor Phase I ESA Exec Summary](https://www.shelbycountytn.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3848/Wolf-River-Harbor-Phase-I-ESA-Executive-Summary?bidId=)
- **Mud Island / “City Island”:** Sandbar/peninsula forming since ~1900; by 1916 connected to mainland with Wolf River diverted to keep harbor open. Northern end generally undeveloped or under cultivation before 1950s; nickname “Mud Island” debated in 1940s press. **Not** the modern river park (opened 1982). Sources: [HMDB Mud Island](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=88265); ESA summary above
- **Beale Street corridor:** African American commercial / entertainment spine; Palace Theatre at **324 Beale** (Amateur Night from 1935; postwar renovation 1949); Handy Park at Beale & Third (park dedicated 1931 on former market house site). WDIA went on air **7 Jun 1947** (Union Ave studios); Black-oriented programming from late 1948. Sources: [Cinema Treasures – Palace](https://www.cinematreasures.org/theaters/14229); [Historic Memphis parks](https://historic-memphis.com/memphis-historic/parks2/historicparks.html); [Wikipedia – WDIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDIA)

### Sterick Building (HQ landmark)

- **Address (period):** 8 North Third Street, NE corner Madison & Third.
- **Built:** Construction began **Nov 1928**; completed **1929**; public opening **5 Mar 1930** (Mayor Watkins Overton). Sources disagree slightly on “completed 1928” vs “1929/1930 opening”—treat as **1928–30 build / 1930 opening**. ([Memphis Magazine – Story of the Sterick](https://memphismagazine.com/features/longform/the-story-of-the-sterick-building/); [Memphis Heritage](https://www.memphisheritage.org/historic-properties/sterick-building/))
- **Form:** ~29-story Commercial Gothic / Gothic Revival; ~365 ft; ~900 offices; barber, beauty parlor, bank, pharmacy on site; tallest in Memphis until 1965.
- **Useful for fiction:** Dominant skyline HQ; elevator banks, basement levels, street-level shops, sightlines down Madison/Third. Period occupants were ordinary commercial tenants—not a government fortress (fictionalize use freely).

### Industrial / dock areas

1. **Downtown cobble riverfront + South Bluffs** — older packet/steamboat + rail warehouse zone; still active mid-century but increasingly inadequate for post-WWII barge scale.
2. **Wolf River Harbor / Uptown shoreline** — primary industrial waterfront for grain/lumber/cotton through 1940s–50s (**best match for Ch1 east dock opposite a north-west farm**).
3. **President’s Island** — annexed 1947; Flood Control Act 1946 funding; Corps construction from ~1948; industrial harbor project completed ~1951. **Edge of period:** useful as “under construction / new port” flavor for late-1940s–1950, not a finished 1945 set piece. Sources: [Port of Memphis history](https://portofmemphis.com/history/); [Wikipedia – President's Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Island)

### Parks (within / near footprint)

- **Court Square** — historic downtown square; survived 1940s threat of conversion to parking. ([HMDB](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=148966))
- **W.C. Handy Park** — Beale & Third, 1931.
- **Riverside Park** — south riverfront park (Kessler-era system); pavilion photos c.1942 exist in Memphis special collections.
- **Overton Park** — Midtown; major Kessler park with zoo/golf; **likely outside** a tight 10 sq mi riverside footprint—reference only if playable area expands east.

### Airport(s)

- **Memphis Municipal Airport** — opened **15 Jun 1929** on former Ward Farm ~**7.5 miles southeast** of downtown; WPA terminal late 1930s; leased to War Department Jul 1942; HQ **4th Ferrying Group** (ATC); ferrying group ceased Mar 1947; city regained control Jan 1949; terminal enlarged 1947. Sources: [TN Encyclopedia – Airports](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/airports/); [Wikipedia – Memphis International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_International_Airport)
- **Design note:** Airport is **outside** a compact riverside 10 sq mi unless the east map deliberately reaches SE. For river-focused Ch1, treat as off-map / radio / cutscene asset.
- Memphis Downtown Airport on Mud Island: **1959–1970** — **after** this period; do not use for 1945–50.

## 4. West Memphis (West) — ~10 sq mi highlights

### Character (FACT)

- Floodplain town west of Mississippi, elevation ~215 ft; protected by **St. Francis Levee** system (district est. 1893); major floods historically (1912–13, 1927, 1937).
- Lumber → cleared land → cotton agriculture; rail + Harahan auto access drove growth after 1916–17.
- Population boom: **895 (1930) → 3,369 (1940) → 9,112 (1950)**. By 1938: multiple lumber cos., cottonseed oil plants, gins, large cotton compress, feed mill, distillery, ice plant. ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/west-memphis-crittenden-county-871/); AHPP 2011 tour script)

### City Hall (HQ landmark) — date correction

- **100 Court Street**, on a semi-circular drive south of Broadway, SW of Broadway / Missouri intersection.
- Architect/engineer **R.D. Eberdt**; PWA loan/grants; construction began **29 Jul 1938**; completed **13 Jun 1939**; opened **18 Jul 1939**.
- Functions: city offices (front), fire department + jail (rear).
- **Game note:** Brief’s “~1930” is **incorrect**—use **1939**. Building is still period-correct for 1945–50 (courtroom wing is 1954–55).

### Key roads (FACT)

- **Broadway Avenue / U.S. Highway 70** — main E–W commercial thoroughfare; tourist courts, hotels, restaurants; East Broadway (esp. 700–900 blocks) forms later Commercial Historic District (NR 2008); most historic fabric 1930s–early 1950s.
- **Missouri Street / U.S. Highway 61** — N–S; divides E/W Broadway; opened/cleared ~1917, graveled 1921, paved 1922; downtown epicenter at Broadway × Missouri.
- Bridge approaches / long wooden/earthen **viaduct from levee to Harahan** over Arkansas bottomland (~2.5 mi historically for Harahan approach)—strong traversal set piece.
- **8th Street** — called “**Beale Street West**” in 1930s–50s for nightlife/music. Documented spots (Encyclopedia of Arkansas): Square Deal Café / Miss Annie’s Place (S. 16th St.—B.B. King early entertaining); Coffee Cup at 204 E. Broadway (1950s—slightly late for early Ch1); Willowdale Inn, Cotton Club, Plantation Inn along Broadway (period nightlife—**verify exact open years in directories before locking names**).

### Farms / agriculture / riverside

- Crittenden County: intensive **cotton** plantation / tenant / cropper agriculture (1940 Ag Census plantation study includes Crittenden). Surrounding landscape after timber clear-cut = fertile farmland.
- **North / riverside of West Memphis toward Mound City:** historically agricultural / plantation leveeside land; Mound City was 19th-c. ferry/trading point opposite Memphis; plantation cypress commissary / sharecropper housing typology is attested in Delta (modern Mound City tourism site preserves examples—use as **typology**, not as a claim that a specific named farm existed at a GPS pin in 1947). Sources: [Encyclopedia of Arkansas – West Memphis / Mound City Road mounds](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/west-memphis-crittenden-county-871/); [Visit Mound City – History](https://visitmoundcity.com/history/) (**secondary / tourism**—cross-check with USGS 1939 topo)
- **Useful for fiction:** Place Ch1 farm on **north end of west playable map**, river-adjacent inside or just outside levee, cotton/soy/corn fields, outbuildings, river view across to Memphis Uptown/Wolf Harbor industrial shore.

### West playable ~10 sq mi suggestion

- East: river + levee + bridge approach  
- Core: Broadway × Missouri commercial grid + City Hall  
- North: farmland / Mound City Road corridor to river  
- South/west: mix of residual timber/industrial + highway strip (don’t invent street names—pull from 1949 Sanborn)

### Open questions / contradictions

1. **Sterick “completed” year:** sources say completed 1928, completed 1929, or opened Mar 1930. Treat as **built 1928–29, opened 1930**—immaterial for 1945–50 occupation.
2. **City Hall ~1930 vs 1939:** Design doc said ~1930; **primary evidence says 1938–39**. Update fiction bible.
3. **Harahan auto closure year:** Encyclopedia of Arkansas says wagonways closed **1954**; TN Encyclopedia says resumed railway-only traffic in **1950**. Both compatible if auto use became negligible after Dec 1949—flag slight wording difference.
4. **Where exactly is “north end West side farm” relative to Mud Island / Harbor?** Arkansas north riverside (Mound City corridor) faces Memphis Uptown/north harbor—not South Bluffs. Confirm on 1939 USGS before locking Ch1 sightline art.
5. **Mud Island naming / configuration:** Called City Island / The Island / nickname Mud Island in 1940s; modern harbor geometry dates largely to **1960** diversion—do not copy 2020s shoreline blindly.
6. **Memphis & Arkansas Bridge in late-window games:** If story year ≥ Dec 1949, fiction-only-Harahan needs an explicit alt-history reason.
7. **UNVERIFIED / omitted:** Specific 1945–50 bar street numbers beyond Palace (324 Beale) and Schwab (163–165 Beale); exact West Memphis farm owners; ferry names; any street names not appearing in cited sources.
