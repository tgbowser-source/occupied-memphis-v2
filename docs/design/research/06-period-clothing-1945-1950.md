# Period Clothing Research Brief: ~1945–1950

**Project:** Video-game hub — alt-history occupied Memphis / West Memphis  
**Scope:** Art/design reference for character models, not a fashion dissertation  
**Date compiled:** 2026-09-10  
**Era window:** roughly late WWII through early postwar (1945–1950), with wartime garments still common in civilian wardrobes

---

## How to use this brief

- Prefer **silhouette + material + color family** over exact brand or catalog SKU.
- Where regulations are uncertain or local practice varied, items are marked **[UNCERTAIN]** or **[INFER]**.
- Alt-history occupation NPCs (German / Japanese) should use **authentic WWII-era looks as reference**; do not invent postwar occupation dress codes as historical fact.
- Mid-South climate drives fabric weight and sleeve length more than Northern fashion plates.

## 1. US civilians (Memphis / Mid-South)

### Shared Mid-South climate notes (dress consequences)

- **Hot, humid summers** (Memphis Jul–Aug often 90°F+ / high humidity): light cottons, open necks, short sleeves, straw hats, lighter shoes; wool suits and coats stored or avoided midday.
- **Mild winters** with occasional hard freezes: wool coats, sweaters, felt hats; heavy parkas / modern technical outerwear are anachronistic.
- **Spring/fall:** raincoats, light jackets; mud on farm and riverfront shoes is common.

### 1A. Working-class men (farm, yard, casual labor)

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Garments** | Bib overalls (indigo denim or striped “hickory”); cotton work shirts (solid or subtle stripe); coveralls for mechanics; worn suit trousers recycled for work |
| **Colors / materials** | Indigo, faded blue, gray, khaki, brown cotton; wool rare except winter jacket |
| **Footwear** | Leather work shoes / boots; rubber boots for wet farm work; hobnails rare by late ’40s for civilians |
| **Headgear** | Straw field hat or old felt “good” hat pressed into work use; soft caps; **not** modern baseball caps as default [common anachronism] |
| **Cues artists should not get wrong** | Overalls + work shirt is the farm/labor silhouette; hats still common on working men into early ’50s |
| **Avoid** | Distressed fashion jeans with modern fits; logo tees; baseball caps as everyday work default; bright synthetic colors |

### 1B. Factory / industrial men (and women in plants)

- Memphis industries of the era included apparel, textiles, food processing, tobacco, leather, electrical—women were a large share of apparel/textile operatives nationally by late ’40s.
- **Men:** cotton trousers or coveralls; short-sleeve work shirts in heat; leather work shoes; aprons in some trades; no hard hats as modern PPE default **[period PPE was lighter / trade-specific]**.
- **Women in factories:** practical cotton dresses or trousers/slacks with blouses; hairnets or scarves in some plants; sturdy oxfords; less makeup/jewelry on floor than office.

### 1C. Working-class women (home, farm, informal)

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Garments** | Cotton house dresses; aprons (often homemade / feed-sack); shirtwaist day dresses for town |
| **Materials** | Cotton prints; **feed-/flour-sack dresses** still common in rural South into late ’40s–’50s; rayon for “better” dresses when affordable |
| **Footwear** | Practical oxfords, low heels for town; work shoes on farm |
| **Headgear / hair** | Kerchiefs / scarves for housework; hats for church/town more than everyday home |
| **Avoid** | 1955+ full New Look volume as *default* for rural poor (Dior 1947 influence exists but fabric cost limited uptake); yoga pants; modern athletic shoes |

### 1D. Middle-class men (street / office / celebration)

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Garments** | Single-breasted sack suit (often wool–rayon blend postwar); white or light dress shirt; necktie; optional vest declining vs 1930s; overcoat in winter |
| **Colors** | Gray, navy, brown, muted plaids/stripes; white shirts for dress |
| **Footwear** | Leather oxfords / derby shoes; polished for Sunday/celebration |
| **Headgear** | **Fedora** (felt) still standard for dressed men; straw / Panama-type in summer |
| **Class tells vs working-class** | Matched suit + tie + hat + leather dress shoes; pressed fabric; less fading/patching; wristwatch more common |

### 1E. Middle-class women (street / home / birthday celebration)

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Everyday / street** | Knee- to calf-length shirtwaist or day dress; fitted jacket + narrow skirt (wartime “utility” silhouette lingering); gloves and hat for “dressed” outings |
| **Celebration (birthday / church social)** | Best dress (rayon or better cotton print); possibly early fuller skirts after 1947 for those who could afford fabric; costume jewelry; lipstick; seamed stockings (or bare legs in extreme heat) |
| **Footwear** | Pumps or oxfords; peep-toe occasionally for dress |
| **Hair** | Soft rolls, victory-roll leftovers early; gradually longer softer styles toward 1950 |
| **Class tells** | Coordinated hat/gloves/bag; better fabric; less repair; stockings when possible |

### 1F. Teens ~18

- **Boys:** often adult work clothes if employed; for “nice,” adult-style suit or sport coat + trousers (no modern teen streetwear).
- **Girls:** shirtwaist dresses, sweater + skirt sets, saddle shoes or oxfords; bobby sox for school-age looks; less extreme “New Look” than fashion magazines.
- **Avoid:** 1955 teen idol looks (greaser leather, poodle skirts as default) unless intentionally late in window.

### Wardrobe kit — US civilians

**Working-class farm/labor (M):** bib overalls, cotton work shirt, work boots, straw or old felt hat, work gloves.  
**Working-class home (F):** cotton house dress, apron, practical shoes, kerchief.  
**Factory:** coveralls or sturdy cotton separates, work shoes, optional hair covering (F).  
**Middle-class street (M):** gray/navy suit, white shirt, tie, fedora, oxfords.  
**Middle-class celebration (F):** best shirtwaist or early fuller dress, pumps, hat, gloves, handbag.  
**Teen (M/F):** scaled adult separates; saddle shoes optional (F).

## 2. US local law enforcement (Memphis PD / county / Arkansas side)

### Memphis Police Department (~1940s–early 1950s)

**Higher-confidence visual cues** (from MPD historical materials summarizing the period):

| Element | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Winter** | Long-sleeve **white** shirts; **navy blue** wool trousers; fingertip-length jackets |
| **Summer** | **Light blue** shirts; jackets often omitted in heat |
| **Headgear** | Flat, circular, **navy-topped** cap with **black bill** (not modern 8-point or baseball) |
| **Badge** | Six-point **star** common through mid-1940s; **1947** change to **pinched-shield** with Tennessee state seal motif |
| **Context** | Walking patrols still important downtown; Black officers hired in number from **1948**, initially restricted to Black neighborhoods / limited authority—useful for NPC casting, not a costume change |

**Special / auxiliary:** WWII-era “Special Officer” badges existed (nickel-plated styles documented in collector markets)—useful for civilian auxiliaries under occupation alt-history **[game fiction may expand]**.

### Shelby County / rural TN deputies

**[UNCERTAIN — local regs not fully verified in open sources]**  
Typical US Southern sheriff/deputy look of the era (use as **soft reference**): khaki or dark trousers; shirt with badge and shoulder patches if any; campaign or similar brimmed hat OR soft peaked cap; leather gun belt. Do not copy modern tactical vests or 5.11 cargo pants.

### Arkansas side (Crittenden Co. / West Memphis)

**[LOW CONFIDENCE]** Specific West Memphis PD / Crittenden Sheriff uniform plates for 1945–50 were **not located** in open digital collections. Arkansas State Police of the broader mid-century period used dark trousers (examples include black wool gabardine with blue stripe in museum collections—date of those pieces not always fixed).  

**Art guidance:** Differentiate AR-side LE from Memphis PD by **badge shape/text** and slightly different hat/shirt color family rather than inventing flashy unique kits. Mark any specific Crittenden/West Memphis kit as **designer-assigned** until primary photos are sourced.

### Wardrobe kit — local LE

- **MPD summer patrol:** light blue long-sleeve or short-sleeve shirt, navy trousers, navy-top black-bill cap, shield badge (post-1947) or star (pre-1947), leather belt, black shoes.  
- **MPD winter:** white shirt + navy jacket + navy trousers + same cap.  
- **County / AR:** khaki or dark service shirt/trousers, brimmed or peaked hat, metal badge, gun belt — **flag as provisional**.

### Anachronisms to avoid (LE)

- Modern ballistic vests, MOLLE, digital camo, baseball caps as duty default, SWAT blacks, fluorescent traffic gear as everyday.

## 3. German (occupation NPC reference)

> **Note:** These are **WWII German force reference looks** for alt-history occupation NPCs. Do not treat as documented 1945–50 Memphis history.

### 3A. Wehrmacht Heer — enlisted

| Element | Typical late-war look |
| --- | --- |
| **Tunic** | Feldgrau wool field blouse (collector labels M40–M43 common; **M44** short “battledress-like” blouse late and scarce—mostly greenest troops) |
| **Color** | Field-grey ranging green-grey → browner late-war cloth |
| **Trousers** | Feldgrau; ankle boots + gaiters increasingly replaced tall jackboots after ~1941–43 |
| **Headgear** | Stahlhelm (M35/M40/M42); soft **M43** peaked field cap very common; side cap (Schiffchen) still seen |
| **Insignia (do not get wrong)** | Breast eagle (**Hoheitszeichen**) above **right** pocket; collar **Litzen** (double bars); rank primarily on **shoulder straps**; enlisted eagle/litzen usually dull grey/white machine weave |
| **Footwear** | Late war: lace-up ankle boots + gaiters more common than jackboots |

### 3B. Wehrmacht Heer — officer

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Tunic** | Better-quality privately purchased service tunic often retained green collar/higher finish; or OR field blouse in the field |
| **Insignia** | Silver/aluminum hand-embroidered litzen and eagle more common; rank on shoulder boards; generals: gold accents, red trouser stripes |
| **Headgear** | Schirmmütze peaked cap (green band, Waffenfarbe piping) or “crusher” / M43 in field |
| **Belt** | Leather officer belt; pistol holster as needed |

**Waffenfarbe (branch color piping) — useful visual cue:** white infantry, red artillery, rose-pink armor, etc. Optional for clarity in game UI.

### 3C. SS (Allgemeine / Waffen-SS)

| Element | Art-critical facts |
| --- | --- |
| **Black prewar dress uniform** | **Seldom worn in wartime field/occupation contexts**; often recalled/stripped 1942 for auxiliaries. Using all-black SS as default occupation kit is a **common media anachronism**. |
| **Default wartime look** | **Feldgrau / earth-grey** service or field tunic with **SS runes** collar tab (right) + rank tab (left); sleeve eagle (not Heer breast eagle placement); Totenkopf on cap for many SS |
| **Waffen-SS combat** | Feldgrau similar to Heer but SS-cut details (wider collar, often angled lower pockets); extensive **camouflage smocks** (plane-tree etc.) more SS-associated than Heer |
| **Ranks** | Collar tabs + shoulder boards; titles like Unterscharführer → Obersturmführer → Sturmbannführer visually distinct—prefer correct tab geometry over inventing |

### 3D. Gestapo / SiPo (Security Police)

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **In Reich / day-to-day** | **Plainclothes** detectives—dark civilian suits, fedoras, overcoats; **not** black leather trench as cartoon default |
| **ID cue** | Oval **warrant disc** on chain: Gestapo discs typically nickel-silver marked *Geheime Staatspolizei*; Kripo bronze/iron variants—*not* a chest badge like US police |
| **In occupied territory** | Often issued **grey-green SS-style service tunics** so they would not be mistaken for civilians; may hold concurrent SS ranks |
| **Ranks** | Criminal-police titles (e.g., Kriminalsekretär, Kriminalkommissar, Kriminalrat) used alongside/instead of SS rank in paperwork; visually, uniformed = SS-type insignia |

### Wardrobe kit — German NPCs

- **Heer enlisted patrol:** feldgrau tunic + trousers, M43 cap or Stahlhelm, ankle boots/gaiters, belt kit, breast eagle + litzen.  
- **Heer officer:** better tunic or OR blouse, Schirmmütze or crusher, leather belt, pistol.  
- **SS NCO/officer:** feldgrau SS tunic, runes + rank tabs, sleeve eagle, peaked or field cap with Totenkopf.  
- **Gestapo plainclothes:** dark suit, hat, overcoat; warrant disc on chain (show on demand).  
- **Gestapo occupied-uniformed:** grey-green SS service tunic + plainclothes face (no comic black leather).

### Anachronisms to avoid (German)

- Ubiquitous shiny black SS dress for street patrols; dual early-war helmet decals in late-war settings; pristine jackboots on every late-war enlisted; Gestapo as leather-coat cosplay only.

## 4. Japanese (occupation NPC reference)

> Again: **IJA / Kempeitai / Tokkō reference looks**, not documented Memphis occupation history.

### 4A. Imperial Japanese Army — enlisted

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Uniform** | **Type 98 (M1938)** field uniform: mustard / khaki / olive-drab wool or cotton; stand-and-fall collar; breast and skirt pockets |
| **Hot climate option** | Lightweight cotton variants; open collar in heat—relevant to Mid-South summers |
| **Headgear** | Field cap (senbō) with star; steel helmet when combat-ready |
| **Footwear** | Wrap puttees + shoes, or boots; gaitered look common |
| **Rank (Type 98)** | **Collar patches** on both sides: red wool base with stars/bars; enlisted rectangular; officers parallelogram-shaped. Type 98 continued widely even after 1943 Type 3 changes. |
| **Branch ID** | Narrow colored strip under collar rank patches (post-regulation changes)—useful if modeling specialist arms |

### 4B. IJA — officer

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Uniform** | Better-tailored khaki/olive tunic; open collar + shirt in heat; leather belt; often **shin guntō** saber for formal/authority scenes |
| **Rank** | Collar tabs with silver stars (vs gold for lower enlisted stars—verify per rank chart when modeling); cuff rank added in later 1943 regulations for officers |
| **Headgear** | Officer field cap or peaked service cap |

### 4C. Kempeitai (military police / secret police functions)

| Element | Visual keys |
| --- | --- |
| **Uniformed** | Standard Type 98 / cavalry-style uniform; often **high black leather boots**; **white armband on left arm** with characters **憲兵** (*kenpei*); black chevron noted in sources |
| **Plainclothes** | Authorized; ID via rank badge or **Imperial chrysanthemum** under jacket lapel |
| **Arms** | Officers: pistol + cavalry saber; enlisted: pistol + bayonet; junior NCOs sometimes **shinai** (bamboo) when handling prisoners |
| **Do not confuse with** | Ordinary IJA infantry (missing white 憲兵 armband) |

### 4D. Tokkō (Special Higher Police — civilian “thought police”)

| Element | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Appearance** | **No unique flashy uniform** for undercover work—**plainclothes** to blend; also used uniformed Home Ministry police dress when overt |
| **Role cue for game** | Suit, overcoat, hat; bureaucratic rather than military silhouette; collaborates with Kempeitai but is **civilian Home Ministry**, not Army |
| **Avoid** | Giving Tokkō the Kempeitai white armband or IJA collar tabs |

### Wardrobe kit — Japanese NPCs

- **IJA enlisted:** khaki Type 98 tunic/trousers, puttees, field cap with star, collar rank patches, rifle/gear as needed.  
- **IJA officer:** tailored khaki, leather belt, saber optional, officer cap, collar tabs.  
- **Kempeitai uniformed:** IJA-like kit + **white 憲兵 armband** + boots; saber/pistol.  
- **Kempeitai plainclothes:** civilian suit + hidden chrysanthemum/rank under lapel.  
- **Tokkō:** nondescript civilian suit; no military armband.

### Anachronisms to avoid (Japanese)

- Modern JSDF digicam; samurai armor cosplay; conflating Kempeitai armband with Tokkō; bright white dress uniforms for field occupation (ceremonial dark-blue Kempeitai dress was largely pre-1942 ceremony).

## 5. Resistance / laborers / dock & warehouse workers

### Dock, river, warehouse (Memphis riverfront context)

Historically, Memphis river commerce relied heavily on Black **roustabouts** and dock labor; period “blend-in” kit for investigators was literally **work pants, boots, and bags**.

| Element | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| **Garments** | Heavy cotton/denim trousers; work shirts; sometimes sleeveless undershirts in heat; leather or canvas aprons in warehouses; suspenders |
| **Colors** | Faded indigo, brown, gray, grease-stained |
| **Footwear** | Work boots / sturdy shoes; expect scuffs and mud |
| **Headgear** | Soft caps, straw hats, battered felt—not helmets |
| **Gear props** | Hooks, gloves, rope, hand trucks—more identity than insignia |

### Resistance / underground (game faction)

No historical uniform. **Art direction:** civilian clothing with practical layers—workwear for cover identities; darker neutral clothes for night; avoid matching “rebel faction” color coding that reads as modern milsim.

**Subtle tells (optional, not historical):** hidden armbands under jackets, shared scarf colors, repaired clothing, mixed US surplus if alt-history allows—label as **fiction**.

### Wardrobe kit — labor / resistance

- **Dock/warehouse:** denim/work pants, stained shirt, boots, soft cap, gloves.  
- **Resistance courier:** nondescript street clothes matching class cover; sturdy shoes; satchel.  
- **Resistance night ops:** dark civilian layers, soft sole shoes, no gleaming badges.

## 6. Other useful NPCs & seasonal dress

### American Red Cross

- **Gray Lady / volunteer indoor:** light **gray** cotton dress, white collar/cuffs often detachable, belt, matching cap; **Red Cross** patch/insignia on sleeve and/or breast; white buttons.
- **Outdoor / military welfare styles:** blue-grey or lighter gray Palm Beach–type summer cloth; visor cap with Red Cross device; ARC pin at collar.
- **Avoid:** modern EMT logos; bright red jumpsuits.

### Clergy

- **Catholic priests (US custom into 1950s):** black **cassock** and/or black suit with **Roman collar** common; cassock still widely worn in this window.
- **Protestant ministers:** dark suit + tie, or preaching robe in services; less standardized street collar.
- Useful for sanctuary / moral-authority scenes in occupied city.

### Rail / river workers

- **Rail:** striped or solid overalls/coveralls, engineer caps, leather gloves, oil-stained cotton—similar to national mid-century rail looks **[INFER local Memphis frisco/IC/etc. without claiming unique Memphis-only kit]**.
- **River:** as dock section; lighter shirts in summer; life-preserver props are era wooden/cork types if shown—not modern inflatable bright orange as default.

### Children’s clothing (family scenes)

- Boys: short pants younger; long trousers older; cotton shirts; overalls common for play/farm; leather shoes or barefoot rural summer.
- Girls: cotton dresses, pinafores, hair ribbons; saddle shoes.
- Avoid: modern graphic tees, plastic sneakers, character IP prints.

### Seasonal Mid-South quick table

| Season | Civilian dress shift |
| --- | --- |
| **Summer** | Cottons, open collars, straw hats, lighter shoes; jackets off; stockings optional/omitted in heat |
| **Winter** | Wool coats, sweaters, felt hats, closed shoes; still rarely “Arctic” gear |
| **Wet** | Galoshes/rubber overshoes; umbrellas; muddy cuffs on river/farm |

## Cross-faction anachronisms (global avoid list)

1. Modern athletic sneakers / logos as everyday 1945–50 Memphis wear.  
2. Baseball caps as universal male headgear (felt/straw still dominate dressed and many work looks).  
3. Black SS dress uniforms as default occupation patrol.  
4. Gestapo = only leather trench + fedora caricature (plain suit + warrant disc is more accurate).  
5. Kempeitai without white 憲兵 armband when “in uniform.”  
6. Tokkō wearing military police armbands.  
7. Ballistic vests / modern police blues on MPD.  
8. Full 1955 poodle-skirt teen culture as 1945 default.  
9. Pristine late-war German jackboots on every enlisted.  
10. Ignoring Mid-South heat (heavy wool closed tunics in August street scenes without sweat/open collar).

## Design decision (2026-09-10)
Period-accurate wardrobe is the **default**. Stylized looks only when Thomas explicitly requests them for a character.
