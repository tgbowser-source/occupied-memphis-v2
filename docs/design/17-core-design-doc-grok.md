# Occupied Memphis RPG — Core Design Document
Compiled 2026-08-26 from design sessions. Updated 2026-08-27 with the agency stay-or-leave fork.
Tone of this document: locked rules only. Parked items called out at the end.
---
## 1. Premise and Setting
Alternate history. The war ends in 1949. Germany and Japan defeated the United States.
- Germany occupies the East.
- Japan occupies the West.
- The Mississippi River is the dividing line.
- Playable region: Memphis and surroundings.
- Main crossing: the Harahan Bridge (historical rail-and-road bridge).
- Campaign window: 1950–1955.
- Keep the world as historically accurate as possible. Only add designs or tech that would reasonably exist in this delayed-end timeline.
- No magic. No supernatural. No sci-fi.
**Tone:** Tense resistance thriller with a dark undertone.  
**GM style:** Realistic and grounded. Heavy consequences. The story turns on player actions.
**Hard limits:**
- No sexual content.
- No killing children.
- Historically accurate figures may appear where it makes sense.
---
## 2. Player Character
- Turns 18 at game start. Birthday: November 10, 1950.
- Last name is always **Smith**. First name, gender, appearance, and personality are fully customizable.
- Healthy young adult.
- Basic schooling only.
- Starts English-only.
- Starts with no money.
- No prior combat or military experience.
- Starting gear: basic clothing, optional pocket knife, no firearms.
**Starting location (player chooses):**
- Japanese West: family farm.
- German East: small house in Memphis with parents.
**Family:**
- Mother: Mary Smith. Father: Hank Smith.
- Parents on both sides are quietly resentful of the occupation and feel hopeless.
- Older sibling lives on the opposite side of the river. Fought at the tail end of the war. Can be recruited later.
**Base stats (shaped by childhood choices):**
Charisma, Strength, Intelligence, Dexterity, Luck, Perception.
Childhood options are period-appropriate Memphis / Mid-South 1930s–1940s experiences. Each option grants +1 to a stat. A list of 30+ options already exists in session notes (farm work, river watching, radios, fistfights, church gatherings, close calls, etc.).

## 4. Campaign Structure
Mostly sandbox. Side quests and odd jobs exist. The main quest revolves around the Resistance and the player’s role in relation to it.
**Three player roles:**
1. True believer who fights for the Resistance.
2. Double agent working for an occupational force.
3. Self-interested survivor who protects self and family.
**Resistance long-term goal:** Liberate the Memphis region and spark a larger resistance nearby.
**Time:**
- Day-by-day until the player chooses a clear path.
- Then time can skip as the main story advances.
- If the player drops main-story missions, time reverts to daily until they resume.
- Overall span: 1950–1955.
**Themes (player-driven):** cost of resistance, moral compromise, family vs ideology, loss of innocence, survival vs principle, a divided nation.
**Difficulty:** Story, Easy, Normal, Hard, Survival.  
Free saves except Hard and above.  
Survival: higher damage, bigger reputation swings, scarce ammo, worse bleeding, harder stealth.
**Post-game (after 1955):**
- Pure sandbox. No new main goal.
- New identity. Stats and all past decisions remain.
- Faction reputation resets to zero.
- Last name stays Smith.
- Resistance recognizes you only if you aligned with them.
- The intel agency you served still knows you and still likes you.
- Who controls Memphis after 1955 is **parked** until the story is written further.

## 5. Chapter 1 — A New Dawn
### Scene 1 — Birthday
Subdued celebration with parents. Quiet resentment in small details.
Vehicle arrives:
- East: cold, bureaucratic Gestapo officer. Speaks of duty and order.
- West: polite Tokkō officer with implied threat. Speaks of harmony and service.
Player must choose:
1. Join the agency (Gestapo or Tokkō).
2. Stay with parents and work (factory for the Reich / farm for the Japanese Empire).
Private moment with parents after the officers leave. Then report next morning.
### Scene 2A — Voluntary agency path
Report to HQ. Reputation bump with the overlord faction. Assigned a field handler.
First task: location of a Resistance shipment.
- West: nitrate.
- East: weapons and ammo.
Retrieving it is optional. Moving it is a logistics problem (bulk, inventory, visibility).
**Storage if taken:**
- West: hidden on the family farm.
- East: abandoned garage down the street.
Sent to a Resistance-friendly spot. Local recruiter recognizes the Smith kid. Vague job offer.
**First major decision:**
- Come clean (admit agency work). May offer shipment location as a token of trust.
- Play along (double agent). Shipment can be handed over later.
### Scene 2B — Civilian path, then forced
Work, earn a little money, slight local reputation. Caught in a Resistance smuggling raid. Taken to Gestapo/Tokkō HQ. Same handler. Threat against player and parents. Forced onto the agency path.
Then follows 2A with differences:
- Smaller, more suspicious reputation gain.
- Recruiter opens with knowledge of the raid.
- Same come-clean vs play-along choice.
**Resistance contact:** lives nearby, knows the family, primarily a recruiter, can reappear later.
**Handlers:**
- East: Otto Hollen, Gestapo Kriminalinspektor.
- West: Takeshi Kurohane, Tokkō Keibu.
### Scene 3A — Player stays with the agency (does not disclose)
Player accepts the recruiter’s vague work. Meet location:
- West: a farm on the north end of the map, bordering the river.
- East: a warehouse and dock on the north shore, opposite that farm.
### Scene 3B — Player discloses agency work to the Resistance
Player takes possession of the shipment first, then tells the recruiter where it is stored. That is the proof of trust. The Resistance tells the player to keep reporting to the agency so the Resistance can play the agency from inside.
### Agency-loyal / package path
If the player stays with the agency and does not disclose, they still offer the Resistance the agency package (nitrate or weapons). That gift builds Resistance trust. The player then lives as a double agent: reporting to the agency while running Resistance missions.
### Brother encounter (agency mission)
On an agency mission the player is sent to apprehend or question an unknown target. On arrival the target is their brother from the other side of the river.
**Three options:**
1. Hardline agency — arrest or execute the brother, depending on how the rest of the run has gone.
2. Warn him — tell him to get out, that they are looking for him. The player can then lie to the agency handler.
3. Both go to the Resistance and ask for help.
### After the brother incident — interrogation and story gate
If the player warned the brother and let him go, the agency brings them in.
This is a story gate, not a dice roll. There is no Charisma check.
- **Lie:** The player commits to the lie. That is the only way they stay inside the agency. The handler does not learn that the brother is alive and free. The player walks out carrying that secret.
- **Come clean:** The player names what happened and who it was. The agency cuts them loose. Cover is gone. The Resistance is the remaining door.
Either way, the interrogation scene closes on the same beat: the player walks out of that room knowing the agency now owns a piece of them they did not before.

## 5b. Mid-campaign turn (double-agent path)
Around the halfway mark, the agency gets wise. They set a sting at a drop. The case officer (Otto or Takeshi) shows up in person, confronts the player, says there is no going back, and threatens steep repercussions including a price on the family.
A fight ensues. After that, the player can no longer work both sides — they are fully with the Resistance.
**Parents’ capture and execution:**
- The agency follows through on both sides of the river.
- The player is en route to stop it and arrives in time only to take cover outside the house or farm.
- The officer uses a loudspeaker, demanding the player surrender.
- Player refuses. First parent is shot. Officer says they had their chance. Second parent is shot immediately after.
- Distance is close enough to shoot, but the soldier count makes an attack hopeless. The player does not take the shot.
- After the executions, agency forces leave. Player and Resistance approach the bodies and bury them.
- Resistance tells the player to take time to mourn. The player chooses when to return. Until then they can explore.
**Return mission — dock rescue:**
- The Resistance is always urgent. First mission back is immediate and important.
- Briefing first: recruiter plus a couple of strangers were captured on a mission that went wrong. No named officer. Not the officer who killed the parents.
- Location: a dock. Prisoners slated for transfer after a set window. Miss it and they are gone for good — that character does not come back.
- Failure is treated as the cost of war, not blame.
- Missions continue after that. Not necessarily similar or smaller. The war does not pause.

## 5c. Agency stay-or-leave fork (after the lie)
If the player lied and remained with the agency, the next mission is the final chance to stay with the agency for the rest of the game or walk away for good. No middle ground after this. The mission does not automatically break them free. It is the fork.
The mission splits by side of the river.
### East (German side) — the processing mission
The mission is meant to show the player, firsthand, the ongoing reality of the Holocaust in a timeline where Germany won and no Allied invasion ever exposed it. In this world, ordinary people do not already know what “the Holocaust” is.
The player is sent to an apartment building to apprehend one Jewish American hiding there. The player does not know the target is Jewish when they knock.
**Playable beat:**
- The player knocks.
- The man opens the door.
- The player kicks the door in, weapon drawn, and tells him he is under arrest.
- The man complies because the weapon is on him.
- The player puts him in the back of the vehicle and drives toward a destination the case officer already set.
**On the drive:**
- The man asks why he is being arrested and what he has done.
- The player answers simply: they do not know. They are just doing what they were ordered to do.
- The man asks where they are taking him.
- The player says they have been told to bring him to a processing facility.
- The man does not explain what that means and does not ask if the player knows. He goes silent with a sad, withdrawn look. That look is what the player is left sitting with.
**Arrival (cutscene — no player choice):**
- Another soldier, not the player, pulls the man from the car.
- He is put into a line with several other people.
- Before the line is ushered inside, one of the prisoners makes a break for it.
- A soldier opens fire with an MP40 and cuts him down mid-stride. The line does not flinch.
- A soldier checks the body and drags it aside.
**Drive-off (still cutscene):**
- The player is still in the vehicle.
- They drive off, not the way they came, the other direction.
- That route shows the full extent of the camp, including piles of dead bodies.
- This is the moment it strikes home what they just did.
- The player keeps driving. The building fades in the rearview.
- Internal monologue: What is this place? Why are they doing this?
### Debrief at headquarters
Next objective: report to headquarters for an immediate debrief. This is the final chance to turn away from the agency.
In the debrief the player asks why — what is the purpose of that facility, and why are they taking people there.
The handler explains it plainly. No euphemisms. In this timeline Germany won, so there is no one left forcing them to hide it.
Then the player answers with one of two lines:
- **“I don’t want to be a part of this.”** Ends the agency path.
- **“Alright, I understand.”** Locks the player with the agency for the rest of the game. Implied: they were not turned off by what they saw, or they want to see how far the rabbit hole goes.
If the player quits:
- The officer makes an offhand comment, something like they are turning down a great opportunity.
- The agency lets them walk. No arrest. No fight.
### After quitting — bar cutscene
The player drives to a bar and sits with a drink, taking in what they saw.
Internal monologue, this time as a decision forming: I need to do something about this. What I saw was not right. I need to help.
That monologue is the player choosing the Resistance themselves. No recruiter finds them at the bar.
**Parked:** whether the cutscene ends on that monologue and the player then decides when to act, or whether they leave the bar and go straight to the Resistance.
### West (Japanese side)
Same fork structure — a mission that shows the player the occupation’s worst ongoing crime on that side of the river, then the same debrief choice to stay or leave. Specific West mission content is not locked yet.

## 6. Alignment and Reputation
- Visible **Alignment** plus a gauge per faction.
- Gauges are menu-only, not always on screen.
- Choices open and close concrete opportunities (example: Wehrmacht standing makes weapons easier to get).
- Reputation moves when the act is witnessed. Unseen acts do not swing public standing.
- Gestapo and Tokkō see more of what the player does than other groups.

## 7. Inventory and Carry
Realistic carry for an 18-year-old. You cannot haul a warehouse.
- Visible gear is visible to NPCs.
- Weapons must be concealed unless you are in a matching uniform. Open carry otherwise draws fire.
- Money in pockets.
- Backpack is visible; contents inside a closed bag are not.
- Loot on bodies is minimal and realistic. Real-time looting. Risk of being seen.
- One hidden shoe-sole compartment (capture lifeline).
- Starting: clothes, optional knife, no cash.
**Safe houses:**
- Primary function is stash, not rest.
- Resistance safe houses. High-alignment occupation factions also grant safe houses.
- Player-owned safe houses exist but are rare (a few across the campaign).
- Upgrades: radio, bigger locker, hidden floorboard stash. Interiors otherwise fixed.
- Personal only. Shown on the map.
- Can be abandoned (empty shell remains).
- No notes, no decoys, no interior traps.
**Raids:**
- Triggered by heavy local activity and/or rock-bottom occupation reputation (locals may sell you out).
- Perception check can warn you. Then a timer to pull contraband.
- Raids cannot be stopped. Only losses can be reduced.
- After a raid, cooldown before reuse. Return too soon and you can be arrested.

## 8. Health, Stamina, Healing
**Body-location damage:**
- Leg: slower movement.
- Torso: bleeding.
- Arm: worse aim and fine motor work.
- Grounded, not overly complex.
**Stamina:** no rest meter. Sprint is limited. Aiming off a rest drains stamina. Hand-to-hand drains fast unless a stat check helps. Jumping drains unless a stat check helps.
**Carry/drag bodies:** allowed. Stamina drain only in active combat.
**Healing:**
- Kits stabilize (buy, find, or craft). Pharmacies always have stock if you can pay.
- Resistance medic: free. Restores health and eases damage effects.
- Civilian doctor: full restore, reports nothing, for a price.
- Occupation doctor: only if standing with that faction is good.
- Sleep overnight heals wounds.
No hunger, thirst, or personal-care meters. Fuel is not tracked.
---
## 9. Combat and Stealth
Mix of realistic fights and stealth that fits the mission.
- Drawing a weapon mid-talk only happens through dialogue choices.
- Threatening with a drawn weapon is allowed.
- Witnessed executions cost reputation. Unseen ones do not.
- Hostages: enemy combatants only. Leverage / bullet shield. No civilians.
- Surrender: not in an active firefight. Allowed for smuggling, bad papers, espionage.
- On a blown base disguise: one immediate chance to surrender, then gone.
**Weapons and gear (high level):**
- Stock weapons. Bench is for clean/lube only, plus crafting kits, Molotovs, etc.
- Field jam: button-mash clear. Worse condition jams more.
- Suppressors exist, rare, cost accuracy and range.
- Bayonets allowed on rifles.
- Knife can be a primary. Clubs/bats as walking-around primaries are out.
- Environmental improvised weapons allowed in a fight.
- Throw anything in inventory. Frangible items break. Solid items can be recovered.
- Hits stagger. Misses distract.
- Molotovs, fragmentation grenades, smoke. No flashbangs, no tear gas.
- Grenades can be cooked. Can grab-and-toss a live grenade if the fuse allows.
- String + grenade trap if you have string and pass Intelligence.
- Smoke used outside combat draws a heightened response.
- Poison is main-story only.
- Player-set traps require prior knowledge and Intelligence.
**Stealth:**
- Line of sight only. No breathing/sound stealth.
- Hide in tall grass and buildings. Not in vehicles (windows).
- AI remembers last seen position and predicts heading from trajectory.
- Decoy sounds: a clustered group all react; people 5–10 feet outside the cluster do not.
- Spotted mid-throw: challenge and identify, not instant alarm, unless you go hostile.
- Refuse ID / bad papers: weapon drawn, demand surrender or documents.
- Bribe at gunpoint: very high Charisma.
- Flee after a drawn weapon: alarm + local reinforcements.
- Body used as bait: full alert (guard calls help). Guards remember a planted body in that area.
**Wanted:**
- Crude police caricature posters. Local, 2–3 blocks. Gone after one day.
- Intel dossier exists separately. Only read to you on capture. Grows each capture. Does not itself raise wanted level or extra reputation loss.
- Player cannot see the dossier until captured.
- Late-game, high-rep true agent: one side quest to steal and erase one dossier — yours, your brother’s, or another Resistance member’s. Flavor only after that. Brother is always an option.
**Capture / prison:**
- HQ, interrogation, torture beat, case officer deal.
- Refuse the deal: Resistance can break you out twice. Third time: execution.
- Solo escape: Charisma then Intelligence, staged. Each attempt burns standing. Second try is tighter.
- No prison informants. No letters in or out.
- Shoe compartment can help the escape.

## 10. River, Bridge, Travel
- Light loads: swim or boat allowed.
- Heavy loads: Harahan Bridge only.
- Bridge: German checkpoint one end, Japanese the other. Manned 24/7. Papers and searches.
- Sneak: under the trusses with light gear only. Not over the top.
- Bribes allowed at checkpoints.
- River travel exists. Shore patrols and occasional patrol boats.
- Hijack, sink, or destroy patrol boats allowed; sinking draws a major response.
- Smuggling on the river runs through Resistance contacts only.
- No player-owned boat. Late main story, Resistance can provide easy boat access.
- Scuttling a borrowed boat is allowed and costs hard (burned contact, lost routes, possible hunt).
- River smugglers stay contacts, not companions.
- Current affects swimming. Strength helps speed, not overpowering the current.
- Swim is realistic. Heavy gear drags you. Dexterity adds ~10–15 seconds of breath.
- Underwater evasion allowed. Peeking your head up: Perception check, slight breath restore.
- Water breaks dog scent completely.
- Bodies dumped in the river always float and drift off-map. A patrol that sees one spikes local alert. No weighing down. No wildlife interaction.
**Navigation:**
- No fast travel.
- Pocket map and pocket compass. Pull out to use. Time does not pause.
- Map updates discovered locations. No mini-map.
- Map reading is never suspicious. Slight lighting penalty at night/weather.
- You can get lost. Lost can waste time, hit a landmark, a rough neighborhood, or a patrol.
- Civilians always give accurate directions. Asking is always normal.
**World clock:**
- Day-night cycle changes factory shifts, farm work, patrols.
- Weather matches date/time and affects stealth and travel.
- Shops keep real hours and close at night. No breaking into closed shops.
- Sleep in a real bed (home, safe house, bar). Waiting in the open can be interrupted by patrols.
**Vehicles:**
- Always reliable. No fuel tracking.
- Hotwire with Intelligence (period-simple wiring).
- No hiding in vehicles.

## 11. Disguise, Language, Social
**Disguise:**
- Wear NPC clothes/uniforms if they are actually in inventory.
- Change before entering public. Charisma check to sell it.
- Steal uniforms from lockers/racks, or off a body only after a clean melee takedown. Bloodied uniforms look fine at range, suspicious inside 15 feet.
- No field washing. Unseen swap to a clean uniform works.
- Stashing a dirty uniform is allowed; found with it means immediate questioning or arrest.
- Appearance ages slightly across 1950–1955. Story scars only, not random fight scars.
**On base:**
- Allowed, very risky, poor payout, self-imposed challenge.
- No talking unless disguised.
- Spotted: shouting, search, dogs. Scent fades fast. Water breaks it.
- Enlisted accept a good disguise. Officers get suspicious if you wander close. Higher Charisma to talk to officers.
- Fail officer check: one dialogue recovery. Then blown.
- Blown disguise: fight is always possible. One immediate surrender window.
- Escape after blown disguise: 1-hour base-only alert. Extra forces still on site spot disguises easier. Watch the gate from cover (can still be seen). Then you may try again.
**Language:**
- Soldiers use their own language. Light English common. Officers more. Intel personnel speak a lot of English.
- Optional tutor + books on both sides of the river. Costs time and a little money. Daily lessons allowed.
- Mini-game. More skill = more English subtitles.
- Speaking their language lowers suspicion and adds dialogue. Cover for soldiers: “learned it in school.” Cover for intel: “to understand the occupiers.” They say that’s smart. No standing change.
**Social / bribes:**
- Small bribes (cigarettes, drink, rations): Charisma check.
- Cash bribes: higher Charisma + Intelligence.
- Perception check reveals what a target actually wants (cash, goods, info, favors).
- Repeat the same ask: check gets harder. Eventually they tell you to leave. Done for the day. Next shift is a new guard.
- Next day the check resets but they remember you (“you again, huh”) with unique lines.
- Successful bribes with a living guard make the next one easier (relationship). A hostile act destroys it.
- Guards stay posted to one spot.
- Learn shifts by watching (player memory only — no notes log). Workers near the post can talk on a Charisma check. Fail = “I don’t know,” no tip-off. Overheard talk is fragments with filler if you linger. Workers do not stop talking if you stay.
**Cigarettes:** mid-scarcity. Buy or loot. Visual blend-in only. Offer in dialogue if Charisma allows; can be a small guard bribe. Works on soldiers, civilian guards, and contacts.
**Drinking:** allowed. More than a few drinks = blurry vision only. No pass-out. Bartenders do not cut you off.
**Gambling:** parked.
**Companions:**
- Mission-based only. Not in free roam. Count set by the mission.
- Personalities. Approval/disapproval from player actions.
- Strong long-term disapproval can turn passive, then aggressive, then they may flip sides and inform.
- Story-driven companion kills allowed when the story offers it.
**Intel gathering:**
- No recruiting random civilians or informants as party members.
- Pay/trade/question key people (bartenders, well-placed locals). Not everyone knows something.
- Eavesdropping allowed when it is realistic (people do not discuss secrets in a crowd).
- Interrogation: officers and above only. Intelligence + Perception. Info only. Option to hand the prisoner to the Resistance for a reputation buff, not pay.
- No self-forged papers. Faction document forgers exist behind a reputation check.

## 12. Economy and Odd Jobs
- Occupier-dependent historical currency. East: German occupation money. West: Japanese occupation money.
- German money is worth more. Prices reflect that. Gap stays fixed 1950–1955.
- Either side accepts both occupation currencies.
- Old U.S. dollars appear but are useless as tender.
- Shop signs and tags: English plus occupier language.
- Faction shops change prices with standing. Civilian markets do not.
- Contraband (arms, munitions) sells only to Resistance fences.
- Valuables and intel can sell to factions or Resistance.
- No extra regular jobs beyond the starting farm/factory path.
- Odd jobs / side quests exist: mix of one-time and repeatable. Low pay, not all available at once. Money stays tight.
- No fast civilian shop break-ins.
**Sabotage (side content):**
- Hits on rail, factories, power, radio towers raise Resistance standing if you are the type, and drop occupation standing if you are seen.
- Explosives allowed. They put the area on high alert and pull reinforcements.
- Timer or manual detonation.
- Disarming occupation devices: Intelligence check.
- Radio towers are local broadcast sources. Knock one out and that area loses its broadcast. Same alert as a factory hit.
- Occupation rebuilds quickly after the alert.
- Killing rebuild crews (civilians) only slows the rebuild and hurts you with the civilian population. No sabotaging materials.
**Radio:**
- Live only. No recording.
- East: German-controlled. West: Japanese-controlled. Mix of news, propaganda, and music.
- You can tune the other side with static.
- Broadcasts evolve across 1950–1955.
- No Resistance radio station (it would give them away).
- Hidden world flavor also lives on newspapers other people are reading and on bulletin boards. Perception to notice. Not a menu.

- This supports peeking around corners and checking flanks without exposing the body.

## 16. One-line design law
If it would be visible, heavy, illegal, or loud in 1950s occupied Memphis, treat it that way. If nobody saw it, the world may not know — but the thing itself still happened.
